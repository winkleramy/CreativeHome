---
title: Levels of Rape
category: Poem
excerpt: Why did we learn to hold the blame but never self-defense?  
---

Why didn't they fight for us?  
Why did we learn to hold the blame but never self-defense?  
Why were we taught what to wear, how to be small, how to deny ourselves instead?

A woman's body isn't their own  
and we know it when we are teens,  
eager to be desired and touched and seen  
as we play at who we are.

We know our desire comes with sacrifice  
that autonomy and want cannot coexist  
that our desire itself is its forfeiture,  
and marriage the ritualized embrace of our vice. 

I don't remember when I first knew  
that freedom and self-expression came at a cost  
that rape is a natural consequence, a fair exchange.

Was it the silence when I explained what happened?  
The averted gaze?  
Or was it simply that it hadn't been me but someone I knew,  
They, not we...?

We all know there are levels to rape,  
levels of blame,  
the signs that she was asking for it,  
the signs of her shame.

But these are my real questions, the one I can't ask..  
Who decided that rape is a fate worse than death,  
that we lose anything of ourselves in the act?  
Am I not the same person before and after?  
Why am I suddenly not worth the same?  

If someone cuts off my thumb, I am still me.  
If someone steals my television, I am the same.  
What holiness exists in my reproductive organs,  
that it could possibly store my entire soul?
