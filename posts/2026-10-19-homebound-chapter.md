---
title: Homebound
category: Moments
excerpt: Seeking answers to elusive questions
---

I am two and a half weeks into this homebound chapter. That is almost two Scaramuchis. I have become an investigator of everyday moments. (Credit to Lance Oppenheim for that line.) The rhythms of the day spin up, evolve, crash, and reinvent themselves. What is background and what is foreground? What is the support and what is the point? The everyday moments blur into purpose, but that unnamed purpose eludes, flirtatiously lingering in my consciousness, maybe there out of the corner of my eye.

Life supports are more visible when life is reduced to its substructure. The walls, the roof, the water, the electrical lighting, the communications infrastructure - never taken for granted here in the mountains. The labor of evolution, the dynamism of nature, can be witnessed in these thought patterns, my mind turning over and over how to make daily life easier and why it might be worth the effort.

I watch as my world makes space for me and as my ego pushes its boundaries outward. I empty the mouse traps, relocate the spiders, poison the gnats. I consume, I trample, I make my voice louder. I pause to hear the reverberation and await the response of nature. Will it accept or reject me? But that's not the question. The conversation is ongoing and reflecting on my role is not required, not even useful. The goal is to go quiet enough to hear the answer to questions I am not wise enough to ask.
