A blackhole of the ephemeral

Starting at the beginning feels like a trap. Starting at the end is impossible, because I'm not sure where I'm going yet. I need to choose somewhere in the middle. 

It's fair to ask at this point - do I have a chip on my shoulder from my experiences with the tech world? I don't know. I do know that succeeding in the tech start-up world would not have brought me happiness, and I am as supportive of myself as ever for leaving it. My 27-ish year-old self didn't have the words for the sadness and disappointment at the time - not just from the Incubator experience but from the decades of brushing up against the masquerade that I didn't have a mask for. Whether it be a byproduct of capitalism or tech culture ego and hype, this black hole for the ephemeral is never satiated, and I gladly pursue my happiness and meaning elsewhere. Do I still bristle at the delusion, faux meritocracy, faux justice? I suppose I do. Why else would I write this, I wonder.

I become laser-focused on a puzzle, a problem to solve. My emotions are triggered by inaccurate information, willful misdirection, and the dissonance of intermingling personal status, reputation, and blame with troubleshooting and discovery. 

I drove my supervisors crazy by metaphorically shooting myself in the foot over and over again when I sensed a hint that I wasn't perfectly correct. I couldn't see that my standard was different than anyone else's and I was duped over and over again by others' self-confidence. 

Her story is another invitation to dive into that well with fuller presence and awareness.
